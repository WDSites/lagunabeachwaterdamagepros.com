What flood damage restoration involves

Flood damage restoration in Laguna Beach, CA is the emergency removal of storm, groundwater or sewer water from a building, followed by drying, cleaning and removal of materials the water contaminated. Floodwater differs from a broken supply line because it often carries soil, runoff or sewage. That usually puts it in water Category 2 or 3.

The work runs in four stages: make the area safe, extract, remove what cannot be saved, then dry and clean. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Floodwater in the house: call right now

Dispatch is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we offer same-day response when available. Floodwater spreads into walls and flooring fast, so a call made at midnight beats one made at breakfast. Calm, specific details help us prepare the right equipment.

Ready.gov is direct about the first rule: do not walk, swim or drive through flood waters, which can be contaminated. If it is safe to do so, turn off electricity to prevent shock. Lift valuables, keep people and pets out of the water, and shut off the main water valve if a broken line is feeding it.

Why hillside homes take on water

Laguna Beach's Housing Element describes steep hillside terrain as shaping how the city was built. Runoff follows that slope, collecting against retaining walls, at the low side of a house and in lower garages. A home that sits below a street or slope can see water arrive from outside, not from a pipe.

The California Geological Survey lists the Bluebird Canyon Landslide of October 2, 1978 among notable historic slope failures. It names no cause, so we do not either. The practical lesson for hillside owners is to watch for wet ground, new seepage and cracks after heavy rain, and tell a professional.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

Not every Laguna Beach home has a basement. Where a home does have one, or has a lower level, a below-grade garage or a room cut into the slope, those spaces take groundwater and drainage first. Water settles at the lowest point and stays until it is pumped out.

The work starts with extraction. Portable extraction units and submersible pumps remove the standing water, and a deep level is often lowered in stages while the surrounding soil is saturated, so walls are not stressed by uneven pressure.

Next, wet carpet, pad, baseboards and lower drywall are cut out. Concrete slabs and block walls hold moisture for days, so air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until a moisture meter shows dry readings. A thermal imaging camera finds damp spots behind finished walls.

Stored items are the loss people overlook. Cardboard boxes, papers and wooden furniture on a lower level go quickly, so carry out what you safely can once the power is off and the floor is secure.

Raw sewage is coming up from a floor drain or toilet: what now?

That is a sewage backup, and the cleanup is a different job from drying a wet floor. The water is Category 3, meaning grossly contaminated. Stop using every drain and toilet in the house, and keep everyone out of the affected room.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

Category 3 water, often called black water, carries sewage and other contaminants. Porous materials it touches, such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are generally removed and discarded rather than cleaned. Hard surfaces are cleaned, sanitized and dried.

Safety comes first. Do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet or if you are standing in water. If there is any electrical or gas risk, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Then work out where the blockage sits. The City of Laguna Beach says private sewer lateral maintenance is the property owner's responsibility, including the point of connection (the wye). A plumber clears that line, and we clean up what came up. The City's Plumber's Hotline is for plumbers planning to clear roots, so do not use it as a homeowner emergency number.

Crews extract the liquid, remove affected porous materials, sanitize surfaces, then dry the structure and recheck with a moisture meter.

Whole-house and crawl space flooding

When water covers a full floor, several extraction units run at once, flooring is lifted and wall cavities are opened at the baseboard to dry. A thermal imaging camera finds wet insulation behind walls that look fine. Our water mitigation page explains the drying stage in detail.

Where a home has a crawl space, standing water soaks insulation, ducting and the underside of the subfloor. Crews pump it out, remove wet insulation and dry the framing. Check this space even if the living area stays dry.

Water categories by source, and what is done

The category sets how much has to be thrown out. Crews decide it on site, and it can worsen the longer water sits.

Flood insurance and FEMA: what to check

The California Department of Insurance says flood is not usually covered by standard homeowners' insurance. A flood policy takes effect 30 days after you sign it, and it typically excludes damage from sources inside the home, like burst pipes. So the time to ask is before a storm.

The City of Laguna Beach FEMA Community Number is 060223, used on flood insurance applications. Floodplain rules are in Municipal Code Chapter 25.38, and flood insurance is mandatory for mapped-floodplain properties financed with a loan. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and ask the City's Community Development Department about your lot.

If you are already flooded, photograph everything and log every call. Ask your insurer which policy responds, since the water source often decides it.

What restoration does not cover, and who we serve

Restoration is not drainage work, waterproofing or plumbing repair. After the water is out, a licensed contractor fixes the grading or the pipe, and you can check any license on the California Contractors State License Board site. Pulling water fast is covered on our emergency water removal page, and lingering damp is a mold remediation matter.
