When water is spreading in Laguna Niguel

Most calls from Laguna Niguel start the same way. A supply line splits under a sink, a water heater lets go in a garage, or a wall darkens after a storm. By the time you notice, water has usually reached the subfloor.

Wet drywall and padding break down fast, and the EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. That is why dispatch runs around the clock.

Laguna Niguel is a separate city from Laguna Beach, about nine miles away. Some ZIP databases list Laguna Beach as an alternate name for 92677, so mail and map apps sometimes blur the two. Our base is at 465 Forest Ave in Laguna Beach, and we dispatch crews from there to you.

Hillsides, creeks and storm water in Laguna Niguel

The city sits on hillside and canyon terrain, and over one-third of it is open space. Sulphur Creek drains most of northern Laguna Niguel, while Salt Creek has two forks in the southern half. If your home backs onto a slope or a channel, check wall bases and the garage floor after heavy rain, since runoff shows there first.

The city's hazard plan describes sporadic heavy storms, with annual rainfall ranging from one-third of normal to more than double. The Public Safety Element maps 100-year flood hazard areas in several locations. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, and remember that standard homeowners policies do not usually cover flood.

Niguel Summit and the 1998 Via Estoril landslide

In 1998 a landslide at Niguel Summit damaged a condominium complex, later demolished, and nine homes on Via Estoril Drive. Repairs included a caisson wall, subdrains and a buttress. If you live on a hillside lot and see wet ground that stays wet, new seepage or fresh cracks, tell a professional promptly.

Older homes and condos: where the leaks start

Laguna Niguel grew from a master-planned community begun in 1959. The city's housing element counts about 6,000 homes built before 1980 and notes roof and window repair needs in the late 1960s and early 1970s neighborhoods. A failing roof or window seal leaks slowly, so the first sign is often a ceiling stain or a soft sill.

Older complexes near SR-73, including Casa La Paz, Milano and Laguna Gardens, share walls and plumbing stacks. A leak upstairs can wet three units, so call the property manager and us in the same hour.

Water mitigation first, repair second

For most calls, the first job is water mitigation: stopping the damage from growing. The crew finds wet materials with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras, extracts standing water, and sets air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. Wet carpet pad usually goes. Hardwood and drywall are often saved if the crew gets to them in time.

Once the structure reads dry, water damage repair covers the rebuild. We explain the scope and price before work starts, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

Extraction, cleanup and flood water

A burst pipe with water pooled on the floor fits emergency water removal. Water that came in from outside or from a sewer backup is a flood damage restoration job, since it is treated as contaminated. Do not touch electrical equipment if it is wet or if you are standing in water.

Restoration is not plumbing repair or roofing. If the source is a failed pipe or a bad roof, a plumber or roofer still has to fix it.

If water is still spreading in your Laguna Niguel home

Do these while you wait for the crew. Stop at any step that feels unsafe.

Find the shutoff

Turn off the main water valve, usually at the front of the house or in the garage. In a condo, ask a neighbor or your HOA where the building shutoff is.

Cut power to wet rooms

Switch off breakers for wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Never touch a wet outlet or appliance.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, books, electronics and photos off wet floors. On a second story, check the ceiling below for sagging before you walk around.

Stay out of dirty water

Water from a toilet, a backed-up drain or outdoor runoff may be contaminated. Keep children and pets away from it.

Tell the dispatcher where the water came from and what it touched. Photos of the damage help your insurance conversation later.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Laguna Niguel?

Is emergency water removal available in Laguna Niguel?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, with same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time, but the dispatcher will tell you what to do while the crew is on the way.

Is Laguna Niguel the same as Laguna Beach?

No. They are separate cities in Orange County, about nine miles apart. Some ZIP databases list Laguna Beach as an alias for Laguna Niguel ZIPs, which is why mail and maps get mixed up. Our base is in Laguna Beach and we dispatch crews to Laguna Niguel from there.

My hillside lot has a damp spot that will not dry. Who do I call?

Persistent wet ground, seepage or new cracks can point to drainage trouble. Call us if water is entering the home, and tell the Building & Safety Division about slope concerns. Ground conditions are for a geotechnical professional, not a restoration crew.

Who do I call if the leak is before my meter?

Moulton Niguel Water District provides water and wastewater service here, so a break on its side of the meter or a street sewer backup is theirs. Water inside your property that has wet your home is ours to dry.
