A canyon that drains toward town

Laguna Canyon is the inland side of Laguna Beach, where Laguna Canyon Road runs between hillside homes and parkland. Only the lower canyon sits inside city limits, and that stretch is heavily developed. Laguna Coast Wilderness Park, over 7,000 acres of canyon terrain, lies to the west.

That geography matters in a storm. Runoff funnels toward the flood-control channel and the streets below, so a garage or studio floor can fill fast. If water is already inside, call first and sort out paperwork after the pumps are running.

Two floods residents still remember

On December 22, 2010, mud and debris flowed out of Laguna Canyon into downtown. Patch reported more than 12 million dollars in losses across around 90 homes and 70 businesses. On December 14, 2021, a storm cell triggered flash flooding on Laguna Canyon Road near the Willow Staging Area, with water also reported in an Emerald Terrace basement and garage.

The practical lesson: water here can arrive in minutes and bring silt with it. Silt-laden water is treated as contaminated, which changes what can be dried and what must be removed. Wikipedia calls the concrete channel undersized for major floods, but neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

What to do first if water is still spreading in the canyon

Do these while you wait for the crew, and skip anything unsafe.

Stop the source

Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or fixture is the cause. If runoff is the cause, do not wade out to sandbag in moving water.

Power down wet areas

Cut breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel dry. In a live-in studio, unplug kilns and power tools.

Lift what you can

Move canvases, paper stock and framed pieces onto tables or shelves. Photograph everything first.

Stay out of silty water

Muddy runoff can carry contamination. Keep children and pets away from it.

Art studios and older cottages dry differently

A homes.com neighborhood guide says many Laguna Canyon homes are live-in art studios and others are older cottages. Older cottages often have wood floors over crawl spaces and layered walls that hold moisture longer than new drywall. Early structural drying with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras finds wet cavities before they are closed up.

Paper, canvas and clay soak up water quickly, so tell the dispatcher what is stored in the space.

Extraction and drying after a canyon flood

Extraction units pull standing water and silt first. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry what remains, while porous items like carpet pad and wet insulation are often removed instead. Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Our pages on flood damage restoration and emergency water removal explain the process, and mold remediation covers what follows if drying is delayed.

Garages and low rooms

The 2021 flooding reached a basement and garage, which shows how below-grade space is exposed when runoff arrives. Standing water soaks framing and stored belongings, so our basement water damage page is worth reading.

Insurance records for a canyon loss

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks often are not. The California Department of Insurance says flood is not usually covered by standard homeowners policies. Check your policy, photograph damage before cleanup, and keep a log of calls and correspondence.

For permits, the City says repairs and electrical or plumbing changes may need one, so ask the City of Laguna Beach Community Development Department. Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency water removal available at night in Laguna Canyon?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7, including nights and weekends, with same-day response when available. Call and describe what the water touched.

Is all of Laguna Canyon inside Laguna Beach?

No. Only the lower canyon is inside city limits. Ask the City of Laguna Beach which jurisdiction covers your property before applying for permits.

Do you work on live-in art studios?

Yes. A studio is handled like any wet building, with extra care for stored materials and electrical equipment. Tell the dispatcher what is inside.

Does homeowners insurance cover canyon flood damage?

Flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage, per the California Department of Insurance. Check your policy and ask your insurer about your loss.
