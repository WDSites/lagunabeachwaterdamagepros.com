What carpet water damage restoration means

Carpet water damage restoration in Laguna Beach, CA is the process of extracting water from installed carpet, pad and tack strip, then drying the layers underneath until a moisture meter reads normal. Sometimes the carpet stays. Sometimes the pad goes. Sometimes everything above the subfloor comes out.

Carpet is really a stack of four things: the pile you see, the pad under it, the tack strip around the edges, and the subfloor below. Water hits the top layer first but settles in the pad, which acts like a sponge and holds moisture long after the surface feels dry. That is why a carpet can look fine and still smell sour three days later.

Why Laguna Beach floors take a beating

The Laguna Beach Housing Element, a city planning document, counts 13,027 housing units in 2020. About 14.8 percent are in buildings of five or more units, and 11.7 percent are two to four units. In those buildings a leak upstairs often lands in a neighbor's carpet, so ask your building manager which unit is the source before anyone starts drying.

The same document describes an aging housing stock and steep hillside terrain. Older single-family homes on slopes often have carpet over wood subfloors with long runs between the heater closet, laundry and bedrooms. A slow supply line leak in one of those runs can wet the pad for days before the carpet shows a stain.

Signs your carpet is wetter than it looks

Squishing underfoot is the obvious one. Less obvious signs matter more because they tell you the pad is holding water.

Look for a darker patch along baseboards, a musty smell when you press a hand into the pile, ripples or bubbles where the carpet has loosened, and brown wicking lines creeping up the edge of a wall. Rusty tack strip nails or a pad that feels cold and clammy also point to moisture below the surface.

What the signs usually mean

A fresh splash that you caught within hours is usually a surface and pad problem. Brown staining that wicks up from the pad, or a smell that has appeared after a day or two, suggests the water went deeper or has sat longer. At that point the subfloor and tack strip need checking too.

First-hour safety for wet carpet

Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet, and stay off any area where water is near outlets or power strips. Shut off the water at the source or the main valve. Switch off power to the wet room at the panel only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor. Keep children and pets off the carpet if the water came from a toilet, drain or outside. Call a plumber for the leak itself.

Clean water versus gray or black water on carpet

Water category decides almost everything. Category 1 comes from a clean source such as a supply line, a faucet or a water heater tank. Carpet that has been wet with Category 1 water for a short time can often be extracted and dried in place, with the pad lifted or replaced.

Gray water, from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium, carries contaminants. Black water, from a toilet overflow or sewage backup, is grossly contaminated. In both cases the carpet and pad are removed, because cleaning cannot make a porous layer safe again. For sewage specifically, see our sewage backup cleanup service.

How we dry wet carpet, pad and subfloor

The method follows the water category, but a typical clean-water job runs through these steps. We work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard as a published reference, and you can ask any company whether its crews do the same.

  1. 1

    Map the moisture

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show where water traveled under the carpet, along walls and into the subfloor. The wet area is almost always larger than the visible stain.

  2. 2

    Extract the water

    A truck-mounted or portable extraction unit with a wand pulls standing water out of the pile and pad. Extraction removes far more water than air alone ever could, so this step shortens everything after it.

  3. 3

    Float or lift the carpet

    On wall-to-wall installs we release the carpet from the tack strip at one edge, then float it, which means lifting it on air so the pad and subfloor can dry underneath. If the pad is beyond saving it is cut out and removed.

  4. 4

    Dry with air movers and dehumidifiers

    Air movers push air across the carpet backing and the exposed subfloor while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Equipment usually runs for a few days, and we recheck readings each visit.

  5. 5

    Check tack strip, subfloor and baseboards

    Wood tack strip holds water and can be replaced cheaply once the carpet is up. We probe the subfloor and the bottom of the wall, and use injectable drying where moisture is trapped behind baseboards.

Stairs, wall-to-wall and area rugs

Wall-to-wall carpet in a bedroom or living room is usually the easiest to float, because it can be released from one wall and propped on air movers. Stairs are harder. Water runs down treads and pools at the bottom, so we often lift the carpet at the base and drop landing, then dry the stringers and risers.

Area rugs are a different case. They are not tacked down, so we take them out, dry them separately and let the floor beneath dry on its own schedule. For deeper wet-floor problems beyond the carpet, our structural drying page explains how we handle subfloors and framing.

The 24 to 48 hour clock and the smell test

The EPA says it is important to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Carpet pad is exactly the kind of material that falls behind on that clock, since it sits sealed between carpet and floor.

After a day or two, watch for a musty smell, dark spots on the backing or the underside of the pad, and a damp feel at the walls. These signs mean moisture is still present. If you see visible growth, stop and call, because mold work follows its own steps. Our mold remediation page explains what happens next, and health questions belong with your physician.

How insurance usually treats wet carpet

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line or an overflowing appliance, is often covered under a homeowners policy. Slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners coverage and needs its own flood policy.

The California Department of Insurance says flood policies typically exclude damage from water sources inside the home, such as burst pipes, so a plumbing leak and a flood are handled under different coverage. Its Residential Claims Guide also tells homeowners to make temporary repairs to prevent further damage and to keep a log of phone calls and correspondence. Check your policy or ask your insurer which applies to you.

Mistakes that turn wet carpet into replacement

Waiting is the biggest one. A pad that is extracted and dried within a day often survives. A pad left for three days usually does not.

Other common errors: running a heater to dry the room (it warms the surface but leaves the pad wet), walking on soaked carpet and pressing water deeper, covering a wet area with a rug, and skipping the subfloor check. Pulling carpet up yourself in a Category 2 or 3 situation is also risky, because contaminated fibers spread through the house.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency carpet water damage service available in Laguna Beach, CA?

Yes. Our dispatch line runs 24/7, and we offer same-day response when available. Call (949) 670-0939 and tell the dispatcher what is wet and where the water came from. We do not promise an exact arrival time.

Can wet carpet be saved, or does it have to be replaced?

It depends mostly on the water category and how long it sat. Clean water caught early can often be extracted and dried in place with the pad lifted or replaced. Gray or black water means the carpet and pad are removed.

Do I have to replace the carpet pad?

Often, yes. Pad absorbs and holds water far longer than the carpet above it. Even with clean water, a pad that stayed wet past a day or two is commonly replaced, while the carpet itself may be floated and dried.

How long does it take to dry wet carpet?

Equipment on a typical clean-water job runs a few days, with readings checked at each visit. The time depends on how much water got into the pad and subfloor, and on how many layers had to be lifted.

What does carpet water damage restoration cost?

It depends on water category, area, carpet and pad type, drying days and any rebuild work. We explain scope and price before work starts, and a call with the details of your room gives the most useful answer.

Does homeowners insurance cover water-damaged carpet?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks usually are not, and flood needs separate coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Photos and a log of calls help any claim.
