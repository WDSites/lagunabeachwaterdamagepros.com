What storm water damage cleanup means

Storm water damage cleanup in Laguna Beach, CA is the pump-out, removal, cleaning and drying of a building after rain, wind or runoff has pushed water inside. It covers wet floors and walls, soaked insulation, muddy thresholds and the contents that sat in the water.

The cause is usually modest in size but wide in reach. A window frame leaks, a gutter spills over a wall, or runoff finds the low door of a garage. The water spreads under flooring and into wall bottoms before you see how far it went.

This page is about rain and wind intrusion and the cleanup after a storm. If a whole street or lot is under water, read about flood damage restoration instead, because that work carries different risks.

Water coming in right now: first-hour safety and what to do

Call the dispatch line first. It runs around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we send a crew the same day when available. Then use the time before they arrive to limit the loss.

Move rugs, shoes, boxes and electronics off the floor. Pull furniture away from wet walls and prop up upholstered pieces on foil or plastic blocks. Open interior doors so air can move, and keep children and pets out of any muddy water.

If rain is still blowing in through a window or door, a tarp, towels or a sheet of plastic can slow it. Do not climb onto a wet roof or ladder in the wind to fix it. That is a job for a roofer once conditions settle.

Storm safety comes before cleanup

Ready.gov advises that you do not walk, swim or drive through flood waters, and not to touch electrical equipment if it is wet or if you are standing in water. If it is safe to reach the panel, turn off power to the wet area. If it is not safe, stay out and call. Water that came from a drain, yard or street can carry sewage and soil, so treat it as contaminated until a crew says otherwise.

How storms get water into Laguna Beach homes

Most storm intrusion follows a short list of weak spots. Wind drives rain sideways, so it finds the failed caulk around a window, the worn seal under a door, or a gap at a vent. Gutters packed with leaves overflow along the wall instead of through the downspout.

Grade and hardscape matter too. Runoff that flows toward a garage door or a lower-level entry has nowhere to go but in. If you see a stain line low on the drywall or a wet slab edge after rain, the water arrived from outside, and a plumber will not find a pipe to fix.

The City of Laguna Beach housing element describes steep hillside terrain shaping how homes are built here. On a slope, runoff speeds up and collects at the downhill side, so check retaining walls, side yards and lower doors first.

Hillside ground, new seepage and cracks

Saturated ground behaves differently from dry ground. The California Geological Survey lists the Bluebird Canyon Landslide of October 2, 1978 among the notable historic slope failures, and no source we use states a cause. We cite it only as a reminder to watch for wet ground.

After heavy rain, look for new seepage on a slope, fresh cracks in a slab or retaining wall, doors that suddenly stick, or soil that has shifted against the foundation. If you see any of these, call a qualified professional about the ground itself. We dry and clean the building, but we do not assess slopes.

Signs the storm got in

Some storm water is obvious. Much of it hides behind baseboards, under flooring and inside wall cavities.

Dark line low on the wall

A tide-mark on drywall or baseboard means water wicked upward from the floor. Wet paper facing and loose paint usually follow.

Cupping or lifting floors

Hardwood edges rise and laminate seams swell when moisture sits under the surface. Carpet may feel cold and damp even after it looks dry.

Musty smell after rain

A stale odor in a closet, garage or lower room points to moisture trapped in materials. Mold needs moisture to grow, so the source matters.

Mud or grit at thresholds

Silt on the floor near a door shows that surface runoff came inside, which raises the contamination level of the water.

Bubbling paint or stained ceilings

Wind-driven rain can enter through the wall or roofline. If a stain is overhead, see our page on roof leak water damage.

Is storm water clean? Category 2 and 3 explained

Rain that comes through a window frame may start clean, but it picks up dirt from the wall, insulation and floor. Water that enters from a yard, gutter overflow or street runoff is classed as Category 2 or Category 3 because it can carry soil, bacteria and debris.

That classification changes the work. Porous materials such as carpet pad, insulation and low drywall that sat in contaminated water are usually removed, not dried. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, then dried in place.

How we clean up and dry out after a storm

The crew follows a set order so nothing stays wet behind a finished surface.

  1. 1

    Map the water

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show which walls, floors and ceilings hold water, including areas that look dry on the surface.

  2. 2

    Extract standing water

    Pumps and extraction units pull water and mud off floors and carpet. Debris and silt are shoveled and bagged before drying starts.

  3. 3

    Remove what cannot dry

    Saturated pad, insulation and damaged drywall come out. Cutting a few inches higher than the wet line is common, so the edge is dry.

  4. 4

    Dry with equipment

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run in a closed space, and injectable drying can reach cavities that cannot be opened. EPA guidance is to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

  5. 5

    Verify and document

    Moisture readings are logged until materials reach a dry standard. Photos and readings give your insurer a clear record. Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

What changes the cost of storm water cleanup

We do not quote amounts on a web page, because the numbers depend on your house. These are the factors that move the scope.

Water category

Contaminated runoff needs removal, cleaning and sanitizing that clean rain does not.

Area affected

A wet entry is a small job. Water that spread under flooring across several rooms is larger.

Materials involved

Tile and concrete dry quickly. Hardwood, carpet pad and drywall take longer or must be replaced.

Drying days and equipment

The number of air movers and dehumidifiers and the days they run set the drying portion of the job.

Rebuild scope

Replacing drywall, baseboard and flooring is a separate stage from the mitigation. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Flood insurance, homeowners insurance and the 30-day wait

The California Department of Insurance says flood is not usually covered by standard homeowners insurance. Whether storm damage counts as flood or as wind-driven rain through an opening can affect which policy responds, so read your policy and ask your insurer.

A flood policy does not take effect when you sign it. The department notes it starts 30 days afterward, so waiting until a storm is forecast is too late. Water sources inside the home, such as burst pipes, are typically excluded from flood policies.

The City of Laguna Beach lists its FEMA community number as 060223 for flood insurance applications and covers floodplain rules in Municipal Code Chapter 25.38. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your own address, and do not assume a zone.

Take temporary steps and keep records

The Department of Insurance residential claims guide advises making temporary repairs to prevent further damage, and it warns that policies may not cover ensuing damage if you have not taken reasonable steps. Mopping up, moving contents and calling for extraction all count as reasonable steps.

It also advises keeping a log of calls and copies of correspondence. Photograph the water line, the source and each damaged item before anything is moved, if that is safe.

Mistakes to avoid after a storm

Do not wait for the wall to look dry. Drywall can feel dry while the cavity behind it holds moisture, and EPA says mold will not grow without water or moisture, which is why timing matters.

Do not run household vacuums on standing water, and do not use a space heater to rush drying near a wet floor. Do not mix cleaning chemicals. Do not throw away damaged items before you have photographed them.

Do not repaint or reinstall flooring over damp material. If the room needs more than a quick towel job, emergency water removal is the next call.

When roads and access slow things down

Storm days can make driving harder in Laguna Beach. Pacific Coast Highway is the main coastal route and Laguna Canyon Road is the inland one, so traffic or road work on either can slow a crew. Tell dispatch your street and any gate or hillside access, and the dispatcher will plan the approach.

What we do and do not do

We stop and dry storm damage, remove what cannot be dried, clean the affected areas and document the work. We are not plumbers, roofers, electricians or waterproofing contractors. If a roof, window or drain failed, call a licensed roofer, window specialist or plumber in parallel.

California's contractor board sets a dollar threshold, covering labor and materials combined, above which work needs a licensed contractor. You can check any license on the CSLB site, and you should.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Laguna Beach, CA?

Yes. The dispatch line answers 24/7, and we send licensed and insured crews the same day when available. We do not promise an arrival time, because road conditions, storm volume and your location all affect it. Call and tell dispatch what is wet and where the water came from.

Do you handle storm water emergencies at night or on weekends in Laguna Beach?

Yes. Storms do not wait for business hours, and neither does dispatch. Night, weekend and holiday calls are taken the same way, and the crew starts with extraction and moisture mapping when they arrive.

What does storm water cleanup cost?

Cost depends on the water category, how much area is wet, the materials involved, how many days of drying equipment are needed and whether rebuild work follows. We explain the scope and price before work starts. We do not post amounts online because every house is different.

Will insurance cover storm water damage?

It depends on how the water got in and what your policy says. Sudden damage is often covered under homeowners insurance, while flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage that takes effect after a 30-day wait. Check your policy and ask your insurer before you decide.

Is it safe to walk through water in the house after a storm?

Not if electricity is involved or you are unsure about the source. Ready.gov says not to touch electrical equipment if it is wet or if you are standing in water. Stay out of rooms with standing water until power is off or a professional confirms it is safe.

How soon should I dry things out after rain gets in?

EPA recommends drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Start extraction and airflow right away, and call us if walls, flooring or insulation are wet.
