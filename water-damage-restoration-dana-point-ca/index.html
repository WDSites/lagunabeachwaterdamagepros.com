Dana Point Harbor, Doheny State Beach and the districts around them

Water damage restoration in Dana Point, CA means stopping the source, removing standing water and drying framing, floors and walls before mold can start. We dispatch from our Laguna Beach base, and the way water gets into a home changes a lot between the harbor and the bluffs.

Dana Point Harbor has slips and moorings for over 2,500 boats, and the city's safety element rates it highest (V5) for sea-level rise vulnerability. If you live near the docks, tell the dispatcher early, because tidal water and a plumbing failure call for different first steps.

Doheny State Beach, a 62-acre state park, sits where San Juan Creek meets the ocean. The city notes that the creek often breaks through the sand barrier there in heavy winter rain, so homes in the low ground nearby should treat any storm warning as a reason to move valuables up before the water arrives.

Creek and coastal exposure in the city's own documents

The City's draft Public Safety Element describes three FEMA floodplains. San Juan Creek is the primary floodway, with a floodplain 700 to 1,200 feet wide, and the Army Corps found its channel cannot contain a 100-year storm. If your home sits near the creek corridor, flood coverage is a separate question from your homeowners policy, so ask your insurer well before a storm season.

Salt Creek and Prima Deshecha Canada are secondary floodways. The same document says landslide susceptibility is highest along the coast north of the harbor, Del Obispo Street, Doheny Park Road and Coast Highway, so new seepage on a slope after rain is worth reporting to a professional.

Floodwater is different from a burst pipe. It can carry sewage and runoff, and Ready.gov warns people not to walk, swim or drive through flood water. We treat it as contaminated until a crew confirms otherwise, and our flood damage restoration service covers that situation.

Two water agencies, one sewer system: who to call in Dana Point

Dana Point is served by two separate water districts, South Coast Water District and Moulton Niguel Water District, and the city's site says to check its boundary map for a given property. South Coast also owns and runs the sewer system. Which agency to call about a meter shutoff depends on your street, so look at your water bill first.

A sewer backup is a different matter from a meter question. Call South Coast Water District to ask whether the problem is in the public line or on your side of the connection. Tell us what they say, because it changes how we contain and clean the area.

When water is spreading in Dana Point, the services we send first

Coastal homes here get hit by a mix of burst supply lines, failed appliance hoses, sewer backups and storm water. We rank the response around that mix. Fast extraction and emergency water removal come first, because every hour of standing water pushes moisture further into subfloors and drywall.

Next comes water mitigation: moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras find wet materials behind finished walls, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure over several days. When creek water or a sewer line is involved, the work moves into the flood category, with removal of porous materials such as carpet pad and low drywall.

Once the structure is dry, water damage repair covers the rebuild. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

If water is still spreading in your Dana Point home

Do these things while you wait for the crew. Only do what is safe.

Stop the source

Turn off the main water valve for a supply line or appliance failure. In a condo or HOA building, ask your property manager where the shared shutoff is.

Cut power only if it is safe

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Ready.gov says not to touch electrical equipment if it is wet.

Lift what you can

Move electronics, documents and small furniture onto counters or upper floors, especially if you are near the creek corridor or the beach.

Stay out of contaminated water

If it came from a toilet, a sewer line or a storm drain, keep children and pets away and do not wade through it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Dana Point?

Yes. We cover 92629, which includes the harbor, City Hall and the Monarch Beach and Monarch Bay area, and 92624, the Capistrano Beach ZIP that the USPS lists with Dana Point as an accepted name. If you are unsure which applies to your address, give the dispatcher your street and cross street.

Is emergency water removal available in Dana Point?

Yes, our dispatch line runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. We offer same-day response when available, and the dispatcher will tell you what to do while the crew is on the way.

Which utility do I call about a meter shutoff in Dana Point?

It depends on your street, because both South Coast Water District and Moulton Niguel Water District serve the city. Check your water bill for the agency name, or use the water district boundary map on the City of Dana Point website.

Will my homeowners insurance cover creek or storm flooding?

The California Department of Insurance says flood is not usually covered by standard homeowners insurance. A separate flood policy typically does not take effect until 30 days after you sign it, so the time to ask is before the rain. Sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe is often covered, so check your policy.
