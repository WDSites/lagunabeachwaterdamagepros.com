A business district with homes tucked into it

Downtown Laguna Beach is more than a main street. The City's Downtown Specific Plan area is framed by the Laguna Canyon Frontage Road, the Pacific Ocean, Legion Street and Cliff Drive, and it is mostly retail and commercial. Residences sit mainly along Lower Cliff Drive, with a few mixed-use buildings scattered through.

That mix matters when pipes fail. A leak in a ground-floor shop can reach an apartment above, and a burst line upstairs can drip onto stock below. We treat each call as a building problem first. Our citywide page on water damage restoration in Laguna Beach, CA covers the wider service.

The December 2010 flood and what it still tells downtown owners

On December 22, 2010, mud and debris flowed out of Laguna Canyon into downtown and piled up in front of the movie theater on Coast Highway at Broadway. Local news had crews cleaning nearly 46 downtown businesses, and about 20 residences were moderately damaged across the city.

The practical lesson: storm water here arrives with silt and debris, so it is contaminated floodwater, not clean supply water. Expect flood damage restoration style handling, with soaked porous materials removed rather than dried in place. The Laguna Canyon side shares the same runoff path.

Flood gates, floodplain maps and what to check

The City's environmental notice says portions of the downtown plan area sit in a FEMA-mapped Special Flood Hazard Area. A local report also quotes the advice that downtown and canyon residents and business owners are encouraged to put up flood gates. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Before you alter doors, thresholds or walls after a loss, ask the City of Laguna Beach Community Development Department what floodproofing or permit rules apply. Standard policies usually exclude flood, so read your policy before a storm.

If water is still spreading in your building

Do these only if it is safe, then call the 24/7 line.

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve for the fixture or the main. In a shared building, tell the property manager or neighbor right away.

Power down wet zones

Cut breakers for wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Never touch wet electrical equipment.

Lift stock and records

Move inventory, paper files, computers and display goods off the floor. Photograph them first.

Stay out of storm water

If mud or street water came in, treat it as contaminated. Keep staff, customers and pets away from it.

Shared walls, ceilings and business continuity

Downtown buildings often share walls, so moisture can travel sideways into the next unit. Crews map it with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras, then set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers so wet cavities dry without pushing humidity into dry rooms. Our structural drying work targets wall cavities and subfloors, where shared-wall losses hide.

Ask any company whether its crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration. A working business should also ask how the job is staged and how daily drying readings are recorded. See commercial water damage for more.

Documentation for tenants, shop owners and landlords

Sudden and accidental water damage is often treated differently from slow leaks, and flood is usually a separate policy. The California Department of Insurance says to make temporary repairs and keep a log of calls and correspondence. Check your policy for business income, stock and tenant improvements.

In a mixed-use building, photograph damaged inventory, ceilings and floors before removal, and keep the drying crew's moisture logs. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. If mold is already visible, mold remediation follows S520 practice.
